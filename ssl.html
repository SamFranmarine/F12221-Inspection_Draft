      Workflow

      1. Select the member. Click any blue UT point on it, or choose it in the member list on the UT data entry tab. One member = one ITP workbook.
      2. Set it up. Enter the Member ID, OD, WT and fit-for-service limit, then the measured length (braces) or inspect-from / to elevations (legs and piles). The rings regenerate to suit.
      3. Initial inspection. Full-length CVI, then UT at 12, 3, 6 and 9 o'clock on every ring (template 1). Red = below the fit-for-service limit.
      4. Record each defect found in the CVI or flagged by a red reading. The tool lays out the detail grid around it (template 3), or the node grid if it's near a member end (template 4).
      5. Take the defect readings. Click points in the model or type in the defect table.
      6. Export to the ITP workbook and save a backup at the end of each shift.

      Scopes — client-nominated members and future phases

      • Create a scope: Scope box above the model → Manage scopes… → New scope. Name it by project or phase (e.g. "2026 campaign — client repair list") and add a reference.
      • Add members: paste the client's list in any format (NW-21, SE-05, TN-03, a whole tower such as NW, a tie side such as TN, or a grid leg such as X3). Matches are counted as you type and anything not recognised is listed so you can check it. Single members can also be added from the Scope panel on the UT data entry tab.
      • Categories: ■ Repair — client nominated · ■ Inspect / assess · ■ Monitor. Add a note (client ref, reason) to each.
      • View: choose the scope in the Scope box. Its members are highlighted, marked ● in the member list and listed with UT progress and defect count; click a row to go to that member. Dim others fades the rest of the structure. Turn the highlight off under Layers.
      • Scopes are shared with the team; the scope you're viewing is your own choice. Export scope list (CSV) gives the list with progress for reporting.

      3 · Recording a defect

      1. On the UT data entry tab, under Defects on this member, click + Add defect and choose the type.
      2. Click Pick on model. The cursor is free: a red marker shows the position and clock under it. Drag rotates, right-drag pans and scroll zooms as usual — only a click places. Esc cancels. Click the member to set the location, or type it in.
      3. Enter the size: axial length × circumferential width (hole, wall loss, dent), diameter and depth (pit), or length and angle to the axis (crack). Save.
      4. Read the warnings. A landing that runs off the member, or a grid within 300 mm of a member end, means the node rules apply.

      Every station is a full ring of 12 positions (the D-S sheet grid). The defect's clock span is shaded on the member and in the defect table. Spacings are defaults until FUZE confirms them; change them under Defect grid rules.

      Through-wall holeHole outline + 4 edge readings (+ax, −ax, CW, CCW) at the edge offset. Exports to the D-S hole table.
      Wall lossZone and landing grid only. Mark the region boundary at the FFS limit on site and record it in the D-S region table.
      PittingPit gauge depth and UT remaining wall at the centre + 4 readings around it. Exports to the pitting log.
      CrackLine with both tips marked; confirm length by MT or ACFM. UT is taken on the grid either side.
      DentCaliper OD at 12–6 and 3–9 across the dent + dent depth, with the UT grid.

      4 · Node template (defects near a member end)

      Chord rings extend at least 300 mm from the brace face on each side, rings run across the intersection band, and each brace is read from its weld toe outward. Node defects are recorded on the D-N sheet. The tool flags them, but node grids aren't generated automatically yet.

      5 · Entering readings

      • Moving the model: drag rotates, right-drag pans, scroll zooms. The same controls apply while placing a defect or point with Pick on model: a click places it, a drag moves the model. On a tablet, normal touch gestures apply.
      • Click a point: the entry box opens at it. Enter saves and moves to the next point, Shift+Enter goes back, Esc closes.
      • Or type in the ring table (initial) or the defect table. Enter moves to the next cell.
      • Layers (top right of the model) turns the structure, deck, orientation, tidal levels, zones and labels on or off. Your choice is remembered in this browser.
      • Point IDs. Every inspection point has a unique ID, shown at the top of its entry box (e.g. N/1-35/1000/12: structure / member / ring position / clock; additional points end /AP01, defect points /DS01/D3/04). Write it on the member beside the reading so photos can be matched. The export adds a Point Register sheet listing every ID with its reading.
      • Blank means no reading; add a ring remark explaining why (coating, clamp, no access).
      • Point can't be read where it's marked? Click it, choose Move…, then click where the reading was actually taken. The original turns grey, the reading moves with it, and the workbook records the move in the ring remarks.
      • Extra readings (around a low reading, a pit cluster, a weld): Additional inspection points › + Add point, then Pick on model or type the position and clock. They show as purple diamonds, AP01, AP02…
      • Colours: OK · below the FFS limit · more than 10% over nominal (verify) · not yet read.

      6 · Export and backup

      • Export to ITP workbook: choose this member's F12221-ITP-002 file. The tool fills the Cover, the Member Survey (setup, ring readings, defect register) and one D-S sheet per defect (D-S02 onwards are copied from D-S01), then saves a completed copy.
      • Sign in with your email to share readings live with the team (green Live pill in the header). Franmarine users are Editors; other users are added by Franmarine in the Access tab as Viewers or Editors. Without signing in, readings save in this browser only (Local only). Use Save backup at the end of each shift; Load backup while signed in replaces the team's copy after you confirm.
      • Additional points go into the ring grid: in the ring's row if they share its position and the clock column is free, otherwise in their own AP rows after the rings (up to 40 rows in total).
      • Each member's data stays in this page until you clear it, so you can re-export after corrections.

      Datums

      Legs: elevation in m rel. LAT, per section (splice to splice). Horizontals: mm from the weld toe at the left leg as drawn on the gridline elevation. Diagonals: mm from the lower end. Hub diagonals: mm from the hub plate edge. Node chord rings: mm from the brace face. SOW Section 4.2 extent (LAT − 2 m to HAT + 2 m) is shown for reference.

      Geometry, member sizes and elevations from the engineer's model (SSL_Substructure DWG, Oct 2026): four 10′ × 10′ towers at the corners of a 40′ square (drawing 63X30Z/M61), each a six-bay jacket from −10.68 to +7.63 m with a V-braced base frame below (none under the SE tower, whose legs are the shortest); tie frames between the towers on the outside elevations and on the gridlines behind them, joined by plan bracing. The model's elevations are taken as m rel. LAT [TBC — confirm the datum]. Splice flange positions are shown 0.27 m above each horizontal [TBC]. Member IDs: tower + number from the top down, the same number for the same position on every tower; tie bracing by side (TN, TE, TS, TW). Grid references follow the previous contractor's report (Section 5.2). HAT (+1.40 m) and the 12 o'clock reference face are open items. Defect grid spacings and landing lengths are defaults pending the FUZE inspection framework.
